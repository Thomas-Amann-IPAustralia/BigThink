# **Director General's introduction**

###### **s35(1)(b) of the Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013 (PGPA Act).**

As stewards of Australia's IP system, we are committed
to delivering high-quality, reliable and accessible
services. Our Strategic Corporate Plan acts as a
blueprint for our agency's work. It provides a clear
connection from our activities to our overarching
purpose: enabling Australians to benefit from great
ideas by providing a world-leading intellectual
property (IP) system.

Australian businesses need an efficient and effective
IP system to support the commercialisation of their
inventions, brands, designs and new plant varieties.

Rapid advances in artificial intelligence (AI) and related
technologies are changing how ideas are created,
used and commercialised, raising new opportunities
and challenges for the IP system. We will respond by
maintaining strong governance and accountability,
ensuring decisions that affect IP rights are overseen by
skilled staff, and providing timely advice on IP policy
as new technologies emerge.

We will also implement changes arising from the
Australia-EU Free Trade agreement, continue work on
our First Nations Strategy and strengthen the evidence
base on the IP system's impact on productivity and
economic growth.

We will continue to seek innovative ways to support
our customers by creating a system that is accessible,
responsive and user-friendly. This includes supporting
Australian businesses and creators to make informed
decisions about their IP by providing clear information,
leveraging data and tailoring our education and
outreach efforts.

Our Strategic Corporate Plan reflects our commitment
to providing a trusted and forward-looking IP system
that supports Australian innovation and investment
and strengthens economic productivity in line with the
government's productivity agenda.

Margaret Tregurtha
Acting Director General,
IP Australia

## **Strategic objectives**

###### **1. Impact**

**1.1** **Increase trust and confidence by maintaining a high-quality,**
**customer-centric, efficient IP rights system.**

and confidence.

We continually improve our processes to reduce costs and regulatory burden for
our customers to make it easy for them to use our services.

**1.2 Increase awareness of the IP system.**

We produce high-quality, accessible and dynamic public education and
engagement resources and initiatives to make it easy for individuals and small
business owners to access and understand how IP rights can benefit them.

We are a steward of the Australian IP system, known for our strong domestic and
international relationships, and our focus on data-driven decision making.

consistency and confidence for Australians entering international markets

Australian consumers.

## **Environment**

Australians' intellectual property is an important
economic asset. IP Australia plays a critical role in
supporting the government's vision for economic
growth, productivity, and job creation. Australian
businesses are looking to the IP system to support
their competitiveness and resilience amid a changing
global outlook. Research and analysis are an
important foundation for our work. They strengthen
the evidence base for government decisions on
productivity, innovation, investment and trade.

With the Australia-European Union Free Trade
Agreement now concluded, new arrangements are
being developed to strengthen intellectual property
protections for geographical indications. This system
will support the protection of European products
and will be open to be used for Australian regional
products, including those of First Nations peoples.

We are making progress on our First Nations Strategy
and preparing for the implementation of the Genetic
Resources and Traditional Knowledge Treaty, subject
to government decisions, to ensure the IP system
better recognises and protects Aboriginal and Torres
Strait Islander peoples' interests. The treaty has
the potential to strengthen respect for traditional
knowledge, improve transparency in the patent
system through disclosure of genetic resources and
associated knowledge, and support a more inclusive
and equitable framework. Together, these initiatives
could help deliver tangible benefits for First Nations
communities while maintaining the IP system's
integrity for all Australians.

Advances in AI are transforming how ideas are
developed, shared and commercialised, how
government services are delivered, and how we
work. These changes matter as they introduce new
challenges and expectations for the IP system. In
response, we are adopting AI in a planned and
accountable way, with clear transparency and risk
controls, including publishing an _<u>[Artifcial Intelligence](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/about-us/~/-/media/Project/IPA/IPAustralia/PDF/Accountability-and-reporting/ip-australia-ai-transparency-statement.pdf)</u>_
_<u>[transparency statement](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/about-us/~/-/media/Project/IPA/IPAustralia/PDF/Accountability-and-reporting/ip-australia-ai-transparency-statement.pdf)</u>_ that outlines how technology
is used and managed, and consulting widely on
broader system effects of AI.

To support Australian businesses to make informed
decisions about their IP, we have increased our
organisational focus and resourcing for public
education and awareness activities. This is in
response to growing demand for clear, practical
guidance to help businesses navigate an increasingly
complex innovation and digital environment,
where understanding and managing IP is critical to
competitiveness and growth. We are increasing IP
literacy through targeted partnerships and tailored
engagement. We are also helping individuals and
businesses build the practical skills to understand and
manage their IP over time. Through digital initiatives
such as TM Checker and IP First Response, and
structured education and awareness activities, we are
helping customers with timely, consistent and trusted
guidance at critical points.

The IP system remains vulnerable to periodic
increases in scam and malicious activity targeting its
users. Recently, there has been an increase in scam

and malicious activity targeting users of the IP system.
As stewards of the system, it is vital our customers
have confidence in the integrity of our system and
the services we provide. We will continue to monitor,
detect and where possible disrupt or prevent scam
activities that may impact our customers. This
includes strengthening our efforts to educate and
raise awareness of scams through direct engagement
with stakeholders such as the attorney profession,
identifying and monitoring for trends in scam activity
so we can respond quickly, and referring matters
to appropriate authorities, including Scamwatch
within the Australian Competition and Consumer
Commission (ACCC) and law enforcement agencies.

Economic conditions can affect demand for IP
rights and, in turn, our financial sustainability. As a
government regulator and cost-recovered agency, we
charge fees for our IP rights services in accordance
with the Australian Government Charging Framework.
We ensure our fees are transparent and recover the
costs of administering the IP rights system in Australia.

Our approach is guided by the Australian
Government's regulator performance framework,
including the Minister's _<u>[Statement of Expectations](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/about-us/~/-/media/Project/IPA/IPAustralia/PDF/Accountability-and-reporting/ip-australia-ministerial-statement-of-expectations-letter.pdf?rev=978687f034834fcebd6f0530810e45c8)</u>_ and
our _<u>[Statement of Intent](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/about-us/~/-/media/Project/IPA/IPAustralia/PDF/Accountability-and-reporting/statement-of-intent-ip-australia-to-minister-ayres.pdf?rev=7347e1b69543468282899c72dd6e6b5f)</u>_, which support transparent,
risk-based and customer-focused administration of
the IP system.

## **Cooperation**

In administering the Australian IP system, we need
to connect with stakeholders at home and overseas.
These connections help us shape the IP system in
Australia's national interests.

We play an essential role in providing advice
to government on the development of IP policy
and legislation, working in conjunction with the
Department of Industry, Science and Resources
(DISR) which coordinates whole-of-government
IP policy. We work with the Attorney-General's
Department, with responsibility for copyright policy,
and other organisations and relevant government
agencies to support IP-related issues in other policy
areas like health, computing, agriculture, design and
research commercialisation. We strive to work across
government to deliver on priority and transformational
work, including contributing to the Australian
Government's Closing the Gap commitments.

Australia's national economic and security agenda
depends on strong and resilient industries that
use critical and innovative technologies. These
technologies produce profits and secure many sectors
including Australia's cyber, defence, space and clean
energy capabilities. We use our data capability and
IP expertise with analytics and economic insight
to help researchers, businesses and policymakers
make these industries more robust, agile and
globally competitive.

We contribute to Australia's system of trade and
relationships with trading partners. We support
the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade
(DFAT) in negotiating IP chapters in Free Trade
Agreements. We also provide support to other IP
offices, including through the WIPO Funds-in-Trust
program, which receives funding from DFAT's Aid
for Trade program. These activities help to create
consistency for Australian exporters who can have
increased confidence that their IP is protected in
international markets.

## **Risk oversight and management**

Our approach to risk management empowers staff to
engage with risk to support informed decisions. We are
committed to developing a positive risk culture where
all staff confidently engage with risk as part of their
day-to-day work and can adapt when responding to
uncertainties and opportunities.

IP Australia's Risk Management Framework includes:

 - Accountable Authority Instructions -- sets
out instructions from the Director General on
managing risk within IP Australia.

 - Risk Management Policy and Framework -- defines
our approach to the management of risk, including
our attitude to and objectives for risk management.

 - Risk Management Plan -- ensures that risk
management processes are efficiently integrated
into day-to-day work.

 - Risk objectives, appetites, and tolerances --
supports staff to identify, monitor and treat risk
and make decisions aligned to the Executive
Board's expectations.

Our staff are encouraged to pursue, retain and accept
risks that have been well considered within the bounds
of the agency's risk appetites and their personal
responsibility and accountability limits. This is done in
an open and transparent manner to reduce threats and
seize opportunities for the benefit of the agency.

To support effective governance, we embed risk
management through the following business processes:

 - risk management and assessment to support
business processes including project and
program management, AI/ADM governance,
integrity, change management, procurement and
contract management

 - enterprise resilience framework

 - strategic and operational planning

 - alignment to our regulatory
performance framework

 - audit and assurance program

 - consideration of risk management as standing
items at quarterly Executive Board meetings,

by IP Australia's Audit Committee.

Our staff receive ongoing support and development to
help them implement the Risk Management Policy and
Framework and achieve the desired level of risk maturity.
Confidently engaging with risk is a core element of our
Capability Framework and applies to all staff. Structured
and ad hoc learning and development processes
support the framework. These are aimed at all stages of
the learning journey, from induction through to Senior
Executive Service-specific programs. We seek insights
into our risk management culture through our internal
audit program and other benchmarking activities.

## **Performance measures**

Our performance measures show how we will assess whether we are achieving
our purpose over the life of this plan. Taken together, they measure the quality,
timeliness, effectiveness and accessibility of our key activities, including
administering IP rights, regulating the attorney profession, increasing awareness of
the IP system, and providing advice to government.

Our regulatory functions are set out in the _<u>[Trade Marks Act 1995](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04969/2020-02-27/text)</u>_, _<u>[Patents Act 1990](https://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdb/au/legis/cth/consol_act/pa1990109/)</u>_,
_<u>[Designs Act 2003](https://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdb/au/legis/cth/consol_act/da200391/)</u>_, _<u>[Plant Breeder's Act 1994](https://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdb/au/legis/cth/consol_act/pbra1994222/)</u>_ and the _<u>[Olympic Insignia Protection](https://www8.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdb/au/legis/cth/consol_act/oipa1987306/)</u>_

including trade marks, patents, design rights and plant breeder's rights, as well
as the Australian and New Zealand patent attorney and Australian trade marks
attorney professions.

We apply the Australian Government's principles of regulator best practice. This
includes a focus on continuous improvement, data informed decision making, risk
based regulation, and effective engagement with stakeholders.

In accordance with the _<u>[Public Governwance, Performance and Accountability](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2013A00123/latest/text)</u>_
_<u>[Act 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2013A00123/latest/text)</u>_, and _<u>[Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Rule 2014](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2014L00911/latest/text)</u>_ <u>, we</u>

achieving our purpose. These measures are aligned to our outcomes, programs
and key activities presented in our Portfolio Budget Statements and support
transparent assessment of our performance as a regulator, administrator and
Commonwealth entity.

We will report the results of these performance measures in our annual
performance statements.
